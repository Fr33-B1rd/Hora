Data and services

Daylight runs on your own machine. This page says what leaves it, what is kept, and where.

The assistant

What you type goes from this page to the Daylight server on this machine and on to Hermes Agent, also on this machine. Hermes sends the conversation, what it remembers about you, its instructions and the results of the tools it used to an NVIDIA Nemotron model on Nebius Token Factory. Hermes keeps its own history of conversations in its home folder. This tab keeps the current conversation in the browser's session storage so a refresh does not lose it; “New conversation” clears it.

What it remembers

Things the assistant has learned about you are plain text files in the Hermes home folder. Foods you like or avoid and the research topics you follow are small files next to them. The “What I know” page shows all of it and lets you remove any entry; nothing is remembered anywhere you cannot see there.

Scheduled jobs

The research digest sends the search words of your topics to the public arXiv service, then asks a small Nemotron model which new papers are relevant. The grocery job reads Lidl Sverige's public weekly offer pages once a day and asks the same model which offers match your likes and dislikes. Digests are delivered through Telegram, so their text passes through Telegram's servers. You can pause either job on the “What I know” page.

Job search

A CV you upload is turned into text on this server and kept in the assistant's home folder; the file itself is not kept. To check postings against it, the CV text and your stated preferences are sent to Nemotron models on Nebius, together with the text of each posting. The job titles you ask for are sent as search words to Arbetsförmedlingen's public job listings and to the remote-job boards Himalayas and Jobicy; your CV is never sent to them. Each match links to the original posting at its source. You can remove the CV, and with it every match, on the “What I know” page. Nothing is ever sent to an employer: applying is yours to do.

Plans, places and events

Planning a day sends place searches, place IDs and departure times to Google Maps. Events come from Visit Stockholm's public listings and news from SVT Stockholm's public feed. The map on this page connects to Google directly with a separate browser key that is restricted to this address; the server's key is never sent to the page. The ten most recent checked plans are kept in the local data folder so this page can show them.

Calendar

Connecting Google Calendar lets Daylight read the chosen day in your primary calendar and create or update its own events there. The planner uses only the busy times; event titles, descriptions and guests are not sent to the model. Nothing is written until you have seen a preview of the exact changes and confirmed it. Daylight sends no invitations, deletes nothing it did not create, and does not book, buy or pay for anything.

Calendar tokens are stored encrypted in the local data folder. The key is in the same folder, so anyone who can read the whole folder can decrypt them: keep it on an account only you use. Disconnecting removes the tokens and any pending previews, not events already created. You can also revoke access in your Google account's third-party access settings.

Limits worth knowing

Opening hours do not guarantee a table, a seat or a ticket. Costs are estimates. Offers and listings can change after they were read. This is a personal prototype for one user on one machine; it is not built to be exposed to the internet as it stands.

Map use is subject to the Google Maps terms and the Google privacy policy.
